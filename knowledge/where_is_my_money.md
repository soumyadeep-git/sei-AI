# Wise Help Centre: "Where is my money?"

Source: https://wise.com/help/topics/5bVKT0uQdBrDp6T62keyfz/sending-money
Retrieved: 2026-11-02. Six articles, condensed for voice; facts are unchanged.

## How do I check my transfer's status?

The easiest way is the tracker in the Wise account: log in, go to Home to see the activity list, and select the transfer.

The tracker shows these stages:
- "Your money's being processed": Wise is still waiting to receive the money from the sender's bank.
- "Money received": Wise has the money and is converting it to the new currency.
- "Transfer sent": Wise has sent the money to the recipient's bank. Their bank then processes and delivers it, which can take a few working days depending on the currency. The tracker might not update again until the money is delivered.

Common reasons for a delay:
- The money is slow to reach Wise. If the transfer has shown "Your money's being processed" for more than 2 working days, check with your bank that they sent the money.
- Wise may need to verify information to meet regulations. If so, Wise sends an email explaining what's needed, so keep an eye on your inbox.
- A mistake in the recipient's details. Some banks still process the payment but take longer; others reject it and send it back to Wise.
- Weekends and public holidays. Banks don't process transfers then; they resume the next working day.

To speed things up once a transfer is sent: give the recipient a transfer receipt. It's a PDF with all the payment details their bank can use to track the money.

## When will my money arrive?

Wise gives an estimated arrival time when the transfer is made. Check progress in the tracker (log in, Home, select the transfer).

- "Due to arrive today" means the money should already have arrived; some banks just need a few hours to process transfers from Wise.
- Recipient details: small name typos (for example "Jon" instead of "John") usually aren't a problem, but for some currencies, such as Japanese yen, they can be. A wrong account number can delay the money or get it returned to Wise; if it's returned, Wise says so straight away.
- Weekends and holidays: delivery estimates account for banking hours in the destination country. Some banks stop processing early on Fridays, so money due on a Friday may show the next working day.
- If the recipient says it hasn't arrived, ask them to check: the sender name (they should look for a payment from Wise or one of its partners, not the sender's name; the amount and reference help find it); that their bank details are correct; and their account's currency (if it differs from the currency sent, their bank may have converted it, so the amount may differ).
- Getting a receipt: the recipient's bank may have the money but not yet have credited it. The Wise receipt includes Wise's bank details and, for some currencies, tracking numbers. To get it: log in, select the transfer, then use "Get PDF receipt" (under the three-dots menu in a browser, or the button in the app), save it and send it to the recipient.
- Money sent from Wise Interest or Stocks has daily thresholds; amounts above them can add a delay.

## Why does it say my transfer's complete when the money hasn't arrived yet?

Wise marks a transfer "Complete" once it has sent the money to the recipient's bank, and the estimate leaves room for that bank to process it. If the recipient hasn't received it, either:
1. The receiving bank is still processing it. Some banks take up to 1 working day to release money. The recipient can ask their bank to speed it up using the transfer receipt.
2. It has arrived but looks different. The payment shows as from Wise or a banking partner, not the sender's name, so the recipient should look for the reference number. If their account is in a different currency, their bank converts it again and the amount may differ.

## Why is my transfer taking longer than the estimate?

Delays can happen at any of three stages:
1. Money on its way to Wise: depends on how fast the sender's bank sends it.
2. Wise processing the transfer: Wise has the money and is converting it; this is usually where security checks happen.
3. Money on its way to the recipient: up to the recipient's bank. The recipient can ask their bank to check on the payment, using the transfer confirmation.

Common reasons:
- Security checks: standard for regulated financial institutions. If Wise needs more information, such as ID or proof of where the money came from, it sends an email. These checks can't be sped up, but Wise works as fast as it can.
- Payment method: card payments usually reach Wise instantly; bank transfers take 1 to 4 working days; Swift transfers take 1 to 6 working days. An estimate for each method is shown when setting up the transfer.
- Weekends and public holidays: estimates count working days only, Monday to Friday, for banks in both the sending and receiving countries. Money sent on a Friday or before a holiday may not move until the next working day.
- Mistakes in the recipient's details: the recipient's bank may reject the payment and send it back to Wise. If the activity list says the money is coming back, you can fix the details or cancel the transfer. Refunds can take a few days depending on the currency and payment method.

## What is a proof of payment?

A document showing you sent money from your bank account: a PDF bank statement, a screenshot from online banking, or other files under 10 MB, up to 5 files. Wise asks for it when the money hasn't reached Wise in the expected time, or to verify it came from an account in your name. Wise emails instructions for uploading it.

It must clearly show: your full name and account number; your bank's name or logo (both for USD, CAD and INR); Wise's name (Wise Ltd) and account number; the date the payment was initiated; the amount; the currency; and the payment reference.

Good to know:
- If everything doesn't fit in one screenshot, send two; both must show your bank account number.
- Not accepted: text pasted into an email, photos of a computer screen, bank card photos, Wise receipts, or password-protected files.
- For Swift payments, the proof needed is a pacs.008 document.
- For payments from Australia or New Zealand, a screenshot isn't enough; Wise also needs a recent bank statement showing your name, issue date and bank details (or just the statement, if it shows everything above).

## What's a banking partner reference number?

Every Wise transfer has a transfer number, used when contacting Wise. Some transfers also get a banking partner reference number, because Wise pays out through local banking partners in each country.

Give it to the recipient: once the transfer shows as completed, they can give it to their bank to locate the payment. Banks may call it something else; in India, for example, a UTR (Unique Transaction Reference) or transaction reference number.
